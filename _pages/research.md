My research uses computational chemistry and data science to understand and design molecular and materials systems for catalysis, separation, and sustainable chemical transformations.

## Catalyst design

I study molecular and materials-based catalysts using quantum chemical calculations, mechanistic modeling, scaling relationships, and activity maps. Current themes include frustrated Lewis pair catalysis, CO₂ hydrogenation, and active-site design in porous scaffolds.

## Metal-organic frameworks and porous materials

I investigate functional porous materials for catalysis, gas adsorption, isotope separation, and chemical reactivity. This includes the design of active sites in metal-organic frameworks and related nanoporous materials.

## Machine learning for chemistry

I develop and apply machine-learning models, high-throughput screening workflows, and inverse-design pipelines for accelerating chemical discovery. These tools are used to explore large chemical spaces and identify promising catalysts and materials.

## Computational methods

My work combines density functional theory, molecular modeling, reaction mechanism analysis, conformational sampling, microkinetic modeling, machine-learning interatomic potentials, and cheminformatics workflows.
